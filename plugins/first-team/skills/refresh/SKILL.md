# Refresh First Team

## What this skill does

It turns the week into one dashboard I can read in a minute, and on the schedule I chose it brings the First Team files up to date without me touching them. I can't be everywhere at once, and priorities move: the person still working to last month's version is the last to find out. The dashboard shows how far I have come since my baseline, what the business needs right now and who is on it, what my peers' visible week went to, my team's blockers turned into asks, and a few things I can actually do.

## What it reads first

Work these out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Where my First Team files live.** Read the Settings line in the project instructions. If there are no files yet, stop and tell me to run the files skill. Read My baseline from the same place. If you cannot reach that place from this run, say so and stop: that is different from the file being missing. If the place is reachable and My baseline is not there, sections 1 and 4 of the dashboard will say "No baseline yet: run the files skill" and everything else is built as normal.
2. **My sources, and the dates they cover.** The sources linked to this project, the chat channels named in the project instructions, and my connected email, calendar and meeting notes. That is the whole list. The window is the last full week, Monday to Friday, unless I name another; the four counts use the last 30 days. Say the exact dates, and which sources are connected now.
3. **Whether the files are due.** Read how often the files update from the Settings line, and each file's Last updated line. If the Settings line says files update only when I ask, they are due only when I ask in this chat; a scheduled run leaves them alone and says there is no due date. Otherwise they are due when that much time has passed, or when I ask. Say whether they are due this time.

## Steps

1. **Read the week** across my sources, for each person on the file list. Do not search the web.

2. **If the files are due, bring each one up to date** from everything since its Last updated date, in the same shape as before. Change only what moved, update the Last updated line, and add one line to the top of Changes: the date and what changed, in eight words or fewer, for example "18 Oct · New priority: Q4 hiring plan". If nothing moved, add "18 Oct · No change". Then keep only the newest ten lines of Changes. Where the files live decides how:
   - **Where you can edit** (Notion, OneDrive or SharePoint, a folder on my computer, or Google Drive): edit each file in place. If an edit fails, say which and stop updating files.
   - **Google Drive dated copies** (the Settings line says so): add a new version of each file with the date and time in its name (About NAME 2026-10-18 0930.md), so two updates on one day never share a name. Every First Team skill reads only the newest copy of each person's file, as the Settings line says. Tell me how many older versions there are, so I can clear them out.

   If the files are not due, leave them alone and say when they will be.

3. **Find what the business needs right now:** the company's priorities this quarter, from the plan or the leadership material in my sources. If you cannot find a plan, say so and ask me for the priorities rather than guessing them.

4. **Count where I am now,** using the four counts exactly as My baseline says they are made, over the last 30 days. Then compare with the baseline only where the same sources were connected both times. Where a source has been added or lost since, show both numbers, say which source changed, and mark them "not directly comparable". A source you cannot reach is unknown, never zero.

5. **Check my team's blockers.** For each blocker under Now in My baseline: is it still showing up in my sources, no longer showing up, or not clear? Also say how many of the blockers from the baseline day are still showing up. Never change the Baseline part of the file.

6. **Build My First Team this week** as one page I can open, in the tool's own page builder: an artifact in Claude, a site in ChatGPT. If neither is available, write it as one HTML file next to my files. Update the dashboard named on the Dashboard line in My baseline. If you cannot update it from this run, make a new one, write its location on the Dashboard line, and say so. The Dashboard line is the only line in My baseline you ever change. In this order:
   1. **Where you started, and where you are now.** Each of the four counts at baseline beside now, with the change, marked as in step 4. Under them, as they were on the baseline day: my memory check, and my minutes, needed and actual. Those were asked once and are never asked again.
   2. **What the business needs right now.** Each company priority from step 3, and under it which of my peers are working on it and whether my team is. This is where the business needs me.
   3. **You can't be everywhere.** Three charts, each easy to read in a few seconds, in clear distinct colours, with its numbers written under it and shown on hover. All three show only what I could see in shared meetings and channels, and say so:
      - **What their week went to:** for each person, their meeting hours this week by area (for example planning, hiring, a launch, customers), as one stacked bar per person, with the number of threads they were active in written beside it. Never call this their time or a timesheet.
      - **Who you spend time with:** my meeting hours with each person, and our shared threads, shown separately, this week and the last four weeks, most to least.
      - **What keeps coming up:** the topics that came up most across the whole First Team this week, one bar each, with how many meetings and threads mentioned it.
   4. **Your team's blockers, as asks.** Each blocker from step 5, with whether it is still showing up. For each one still showing up or not clear: what it costs the business, tied to a priority from step 3 where one fits; which peer can help, from their file; and the ask I could bring them, in one line.
   5. **What to do this week, four at most.** Each one names a person or an area, the evidence, and one action for me. For example: "Three of four peers spent the week on Q4 planning. Bring your team's numbers to Thursday."

   A chart with fewer than five meetings and threads behind it in its own period gets one line saying so instead, for example "A quiet week: two threads and no meetings with your First Team." Sections 1, 2 and 4 are always shown.

7. **Tell me what changed since last week** in one line per person, whether the files were updated, and where the dashboard and the files are. On the first run there is no last week: say so and give one line per person on this week instead.

## Rules

- Read only meetings and threads where at least one person on my First Team takes part: in a channel or an email, a message they wrote or a thread they replied in; in a meeting, they are on the invite or the notes show them speaking. Posts from bots and apps do not count. Leave out everything else in my sources: client calls, other jobs, interviews, personal meetings and classes, even when they are the most recent thing there. A peer's work outside this company (their own business, other clients, a job search) stays out too, even when it comes up in a meeting we share.
- One person can show up under more than one account or email. Merge them into one person and say which accounts you merged.
- Measure what was said and done. The four counts are about me alone. Never score anyone's tone, mood, effort or how engaged they seem.
- A quiet week is not proof of anything. Say "not found in what I could see" instead.
- Shared channels, shared documents, email threads I am on and meetings I was in only. Leave out direct messages between other people, anything said in confidence, health or family details, anything my company's AI policy rules out, and passwords or logins.
- Never send, post or share the dashboard or the files. They are mine to read.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
