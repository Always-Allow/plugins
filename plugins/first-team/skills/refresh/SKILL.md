# Refresh First Team

## What this skill does

It turns the week into one dashboard I can read in a minute, and on the schedule I chose it brings the First Team files up to date without me touching them. I can't be everywhere at once, and priorities move: the person still working to last month's version is the last to find out. The dashboard shows how far I have come since my baseline, what the business needs right now and who is on it, where my peers' time and talk went, my team's blockers turned into asks, and a few things I can actually do.

## What it reads first

Work these out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Where my First Team files live.** Read the Settings line in the project instructions. If there are no files yet, stop and tell me to run the files skill. Read My baseline from the same place; if there is none, say the first section will be empty until I run the files skill again.
2. **My sources, and the dates they cover.** The sources linked to this project, the chat channels named in the project instructions, and my connected email, calendar and meeting notes. That is the whole list. The window is the last full week, Monday to Friday, unless I name another; the counts in step 4 use the last 30 days. Say the exact dates.
3. **Whether the files are due.** Read how often the files update from the Settings line, and each file's Last updated line. If the Settings line says files update only when I ask, they are due only when I ask in this chat; a scheduled run leaves them alone and says there is no due date. Otherwise they are due when that much time has passed, or when I ask. Say whether they are due this time.

## Steps

1. **Read the week** across my sources, for each person on the file list. Do not search the web.

2. **If the files are due, bring each one up to date** from everything since its Last updated date, in the same shape as before. Change only what moved, update the Last updated line, and add one line to the top of Changes: the date and what changed, in eight words or fewer, for example "18 Oct · New priority: Q4 hiring plan". If nothing moved, add "18 Oct · No change". Then keep only the newest ten lines of Changes. Where the files live decides how:
   - **Notion, OneDrive or SharePoint, a folder on my computer, or Google Drive where you can edit:** edit each file in place.
   - **Google Drive dated copies** (the Settings line says so): add a new version of each file with the date and time in its name (About NAME 2026-10-18 0930.md), so two updates on one day never share a name. Every First Team skill reads only the newest copy of each person's file, as the Settings line says. Tell me how many older versions there are, so I can clear them out.

   If the files are not due, leave them alone and say when they will be. If you cannot reach where they are kept from this run, say so and tell me to run refresh myself.

3. **Find what the business needs right now:** the company's priorities this quarter, from the plan or the leadership material in my sources. If you cannot find a plan, say so and ask me for the priorities rather than guessing them.

4. **Count where I am now,** the same four counts as My baseline, over the last 30 days and built the same way:
   - **Asks answered:** questions or requests a person on my First Team put to me in a shared channel, email or meeting, how many I answered, and the usual time to answer.
   - **Promises kept:** things I said I would send, check, bring or decide, and how many show up afterwards in my sources.
   - **Time with each person:** meetings and shared threads I had with each of them.
   - **The business above my function:** of the points I raised in leadership meetings and the leadership channel, how many were about a company priority or a peer's area rather than only my own function. An estimate.

5. **Build My First Team this week** as one page I can open, in the tool's own page builder: an artifact in Claude, a site in ChatGPT. If neither is available, write it as one HTML file next to my files and tell me where. Update the same dashboard every week rather than making a new one; if you cannot reach last week's from this run, make a new one, say so, and give me the line for the Settings line. In this order:
   1. **Where you started, and where you are now.** Each of the four counts at baseline beside now, with the change. Under them, as they were on the baseline day: my memory check, and my minutes, needed and actual. Those were asked once and are never asked again.
   2. **What the business needs right now.** Each company priority from step 3, and under it which of my peers are working on it and whether my team is. This is where the business needs me.
   3. **You can't be everywhere.** Three charts, each easy to read in a few seconds, in clear distinct colours, with its numbers written under it and shown on hover:
      - **Where their time went:** for each person, their meetings and the threads they were active in this week, split by area (for example planning, hiring, a launch, customers), as one stacked bar per person. Label it as an estimate from what I could see, never a timesheet.
      - **Who you spend time with:** my meetings and shared threads with each person, this week and the last four weeks, most to least.
      - **What keeps coming up:** the topics that came up most across the whole First Team this week, one bar each, with how many meetings and threads mentioned it.
   4. **Your team's blockers, as asks.** My team's top three blockers from My baseline. For each: what it costs the business, tied to a priority from step 3 where one fits; which peer can help, from their file; and the ask I could bring them, in one line.
   5. **What to do this week, four at most.** Each one names a person or an area, the evidence, and one action for me. For example: "Three of four peers spent the week on Q4 planning. Bring your team's numbers to Thursday."

   A chart or a section with too little behind it (fewer than five meetings and threads this week) gets one line saying so, for example "A quiet week: two threads and no meetings with your First Team", and no chart.

6. **Tell me what changed since last week** in one line per person, whether the files were updated, and where the dashboard and the files are. On the first run there is no last week: say so and give one line per person on this week instead.

## Rules

- Read only meetings and threads where at least one person on my First Team takes part: in a channel, a message they wrote or a thread they replied in; in a meeting, they are on the invite or the notes show them speaking. Posts from bots and apps do not count. Leave out everything else in my sources: client calls, other jobs, interviews, personal meetings and classes, even when they are the most recent thing there.
- A peer's work outside this company (their own business, other clients, a job search) stays out, even when it comes up in a meeting we share.
- One person can show up under more than one account or email. Merge them into one person and say which accounts you merged.
- Measure what was said and done. The four counts are about me alone. Never score anyone's tone, mood, effort or how engaged they seem.
- A quiet week is not proof of anything. Say "not found in what I could see" instead.
- A share of time is an estimate from what you could see. Say what it was built from, and never present it as a timesheet.
- Never change My baseline, apart from reading my team's blockers from it.
- Shared channels, shared documents and meetings I was in only. Leave out direct messages between other people, anything said in confidence, health or family details, anything my company's AI policy rules out, and passwords or logins.
- Never send, post or share the dashboard or the files. They are mine to read.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
